The Cast Iron Care Guide

A cast-iron pan can outlive its owner. It asks for very little: keep it dry, keep it lightly oiled and keep cooking in it.

What seasoning is

Seasoning is not a coating you buy. It is a thin layer of oil that has been heated until it bonds to the iron and hardens, like a natural lacquer. Every time you cook with fat, a little more is added. A well-seasoned pan is black, smooth and close to non-stick.

Everyday cleaning

  1. Clean the pan while it is still warm.
  2. Rinse with hot water and scrub with a brush or a scraper. A small drop of mild washing-up liquid is fine.
  3. For stuck food, scrub with a handful of coarse salt and a little water.
  4. Dry it immediately and completely, then warm it on the hob for a minute to drive off the last moisture.
  5. Wipe a few drops of oil over the inside with kitchen paper, then wipe off as much as you can.

Never soak cast iron, never put it in the dishwasher and never leave it to drip dry.

Seasoning from scratch

New pans are usually seasoned at the factory, but a stripped, rusty or second-hand pan may need it done again.

  1. Scrub off any rust with steel wool until the grey metal shows. Wash, rinse and dry thoroughly on the hob.
  2. Rub a very thin coat of neutral oil over every surface, inside and out, including the handle.
  3. Wipe it back with clean paper as if you had used too much. A thick coat turns sticky.
  4. Bake upside down at about 230°C for an hour, with a tray below to catch drips.
  5. Let it cool in the oven. Repeat three or four times for a strong base layer.

Foods that build seasoning

Anything cooked in fat helps: shallow-frying, searing meat, roasting potatoes, cornbread. In a new pan, avoid long-simmered tomato sauces, vinegar-based marinades until the seasoning is well established, since acid can strip a thin layer. A mature pan copes with them happily for short cooking times.

Storing your pan

Keep it somewhere dry, with the lid off or a piece of kitchen paper inside to absorb moisture. If you stack pans, put a cloth or paper between them to protect the surface. A pan used every few days rarely needs any more attention than this.

Living with cast iron

Cast iron heats slowly and unevenly at first, so give it five minutes on medium before turning the heat up. Once hot it stays hot, which is why it is so good for searing. The handle gets hot too; keep a cloth or handle cover close by.
